How this verifier works

Green means every piece of content the receipt shows is tied, link by link, to a Bitcoin block header that carries real work. The links:

  1. Content to commitment. The text shown (or the Treasure envelope.json) hashes to hash_text. A hidden Sealed or Time Capsule entry shows no content and claims none; its owner can decrypt and check it on The Tower's decrypt page.
  2. Commitment to leaf. hash_text, the carve timestamp and the carver's identity hash to hash_bound. Only two formats are accepted (B-hash for older carves, B-v3 for new ones); see docs/formats.md.
  3. Leaf to root. The Merkle path from hash_bound reaches the batch root.
  4. Root to header. The OpenTimestamps proof, parsed inline, ends at the Merkle root inside the bundled 80-byte Bitcoin block header.
  5. The header is real. Its proof of work and timestamp are checked against floors pinned from the real blocks The Tower has anchored: an absolute difficulty ceiling and a minimum date, which the header itself carries. The minimum block height is checked against the height the timestamp proof states; an 80-byte header does not prove its own height.

Amber means something the receipt shows or implies is not proven. Depending on what is missing, the Bitcoin anchor may or may not have been checked; the result says which (for example: not yet anchored, no timestamp proof bundled, identity withheld on a public receipt, a Treasure envelope not supplied). Red means a claim in the receipt is false or malformed. A field that is checked and fails is always red, even when something else is also missing.

The block time shown is the timestamp in the Bitcoin block header; a miner sets it, and Bitcoin keeps it within about two hours of real time. The carve time written in the receipt is shown only as "stated by the receipt", and only when it is bound into the leaf.

What green does not prove

Offline, the verifier proves the header carries real launch-era Bitcoin work. It cannot prove the block is in the main chain; use the explorer link on a green result for that one-click check. Forging a header that passes would need mining a block at real Bitcoin difficulty.

Want to verify offline with a CLI instead?

The ZIP's README.txt contains step-by-step instructions for the standard opentimestamps-client CLI. That path works on any air-gapped machine with the OTS client installed.
